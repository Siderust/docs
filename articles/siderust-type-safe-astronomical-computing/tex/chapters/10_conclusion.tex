\section{Conclusions}

Years of development in astronomical software have produced specialized libraries whose composition
requires consistent scientific representations. Siderust connects independently reusable quantity,
geometry and time types through astronomical interfaces that retain units, scales, frames, centers
and observer-body identities. Explicit runtime contexts supply the selected models and data.

The Antares altitude-event search and Earth-from-Moon observation exercise this composition. Five
compiler controls reject incompatible representations, while runtime controls distinguish invalid
values and unavailable data. ERFA and CSPICE comparisons meet the declared acceptance criteria for
the evaluated time, reference-system, same-kernel ephemeris and surface-geometry calculations. The
Earth-from-Moon workflow combines DE440s with analytical lunar orientation and retains the
approximately $\EarthParallax^\circ$ altitude shift caused by the observer's finite displacement.
Separate VSOP87A and ELP2000-82B comparisons retain the distinction between implementation
residuals and physical-model differences.

The evaluated workflows compose independently reusable scientific libraries through typed
astronomical interfaces that retain units, time scales, reference frames, coordinate centers and
observer-body identities. Compile-time contracts constrain compatible representations, runtime
validation checks values and data coverage, and ERFA and CSPICE comparisons establish numerical
consistency under the specified models.
